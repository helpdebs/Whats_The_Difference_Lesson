Six families, thirty-six expressions, and not a single gap to fill in. Every task on this page is answered out loud, in a full sentence, about your own life. Tick an expression below the moment it actually leaves your mouth.

01warm-up · 6 min

Sixty seconds each

One question per family. Sixty seconds of talking, no notes, no restarting. If you finish early, keep going until the clock does.

  1. Who are you still meaning to follow up with — and what's stopping you?family 01
  2. What are you completely out of the loop on at the moment?family 02
  3. Tell me about the last time your mind went blank in front of someone.family 03
  4. What are you still trying to figure out this week?family 04
  5. What did you let slide recently when you had every right to react?family 05
  6. Who in your life takes everything in stride? Prove it with a story.family 06

If she stalls: don't supply the expression. Ask a smaller question inside the same story — who was in the room? what did you say first? — and let her arrive at it.

02repair · 7 min

Wrong on purpose

These sentences are read out to you one at a time. Say the English version out loud, then say why the original doesn't work. The second half is the part that matters.

"I charged him about the report."money only
"I'm outside of this subject."invented
"My mind went white."the colour
"We'll manage to solve it somehow."nobody says it
"Forget about this."an order
"She didn't take it seriously."careless, not calm
"I'm out of the loop about this project."preposition
"She took it with stride."preposition
"I'll follow up the invoice tomorrow."with / on
"Work out it before Friday."pronoun position

Rule for this task: a correct repair with no explanation counts as half. She has to name what the wrong version was actually saying.

04role-play · 15 min

Two voices

Four conversations, played through properly — you take A, I take B. Each one has a move you have to make inside it. Nobody stops to check whether it was right until the scene is over.

A freelancer, three weeks unpaidB the client who keeps saying next week

The invoice

You've already sent one polite message. Nothing. Now you're on a call with him.

Move: start neutral and end impatient — follow up on at the beginning, chase up by the end, in the same conversation.

A back from two weeks awayB the colleague who ran everything

The handover

Three decisions were made while you were gone and you've heard about none of them.

Move: fix the past, then protect the future — ask to be brought up to speed, and don't end the call without asking to be kept in the loop.

A youB the colleague who booked the same week

The double booking

You both asked for the last week of December. One of you has to move.

Move: land on an agreement where neither of you walks away losing. Don't say the word problem once.

A youB the one who keeps bringing up March

The same argument, twice

Play the scene once at home with your brother, then again at work with a colleague. Same content, same goal.

Move: close the topic both times. Notice what drop it costs you at work, and what let's leave it there sounds like at home.

Do not correct mid-scene. Note what came out, finish the conversation, then replay the thirty seconds that went wrong.

05free · 8 min

Three minutes, five expressions

Tell me about a week at work that went wrong — a real one. Three minutes, no stopping. Five expressions from this lesson have to land inside the story.

The condition: they have to belong to the sentence. Anything that sounds inserted doesn't count, and I'll tell you which one it was.

3:00

Tick as they land, not afterwards. Five chips lit at the end of the story is the pass mark; four naturals beat six forced.

06close · 5 min

Say it about you

Six lines, finished out loud with something true. No writing — the point is that it comes out at speaking speed.

  • I've been meaning to follow up with .
  • I'm a bit out of the loop on .
  • My mind went blank when .
  • I'm still trying to figure out .
  • I let it slide because .
  • takes everything in stride.

Before next class: pick one expression — one — and use it on purpose in real life. Text it, say it, put it in an email. Then tell me where it landed.
